Invited Workshop at Shanghai International Studies University: "Breaking AI Dependence: Critical Listening and Speaking through AI Output Validation Tasks"

I had the pleasure of visiting the College of International Education at Shanghai International Studies University (SISU) for an invited academic workshop on breaking AI dependence in the language classroom, hosted by Song Yanan, head of the Curriculum and Pedagogy program, with graduate students in attendance.

The session introduced three paradigms for human-AI interaction in the classroom — AI-led, AI-and-teacher co-led, and a three-way AI-teacher-student model — and focused on hands-on practice with the third: teachers provide a source text, AI generates an essay from it, and students collaboratively evaluate the AI's output for factual errors, shifts in meaning, and overall argument quality. Using Chinese myths (the Jade Rabbit, Chang'e, and the Cowherd and Weaver Girl) as source material, participants worked through a tiered task sequence, from spotting factual errors to evaluating structure and cohesion, and discussed how to scaffold similar tasks for foreign-language classrooms with more limited technology access.

Thank you to the College of International Education at SISU for the invitation, and to everyone who joined the discussion — it was a lively and thoughtful session.

New Publication: "AI-Powered Teaching: Reflective Professional Development for GenAI Integration in Mexican EFL/ESL Contexts"

Excited to share that our article about peer observation cycles for teacher training on AI pedagogies has been published online in TESOL Communications. This has been a labor of love over a year in the making, born out of a Citizen Diplomacy Action Fund project with my incredible colleagues and co-organizers Maricarmen Perez, Sussan Róo y Sánchez, Niurka Isabel Passalacqua Olivera, and Luis Prezas Vera.

The article explores our reflections as teachers and teacher trainers determining best practices for integrating AI in our classrooms in ways that are ethical, student-centered, and humanistic.

This work grew out of a presentation at last year's International TESOL Union Conference in Hong Kong, and I'll be back in Singapore this year presenting on my L2 writing chatbot benchmarked by agentive AI. Would love to connect if you'll be there!

Always looking for collaborators. Please feel free to reach out if you're interested in replicating this study in your own context or would like access to the materials we used. The approach is adaptable for teacher trainers across contexts.

New Publication: "Analyzing Movie Posters Across Cultures"

My lesson plan, "Analyzing Movie Posters Across Cultures," is officially published in New Ways in Teaching Visual Literacy, a new volume from TESOL Press.

The lesson uses visual literacy strategies to develop rhetorical L2 writing skills, guiding students to read movie posters as cultural artifacts and translate those analytical insights into structured academic writing. I offer this as a faculty development workshop for instructors interested in task-based approaches to visual literacy and L2 writing instruction.

I've brought this task to life in two contexts: with multilingual learners at NYU Shanghai, and at invited talks at universities, where it has resonated with educators looking for fresh, multimodal approaches to L2 writing.

Contributing to the New Ways in Teaching series, a staple of my practice since I started teaching nearly 15 years ago, feels like a milestone.
